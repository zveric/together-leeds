Before you report

If it is safe, note what happened, when and where, and any witnesses. Keep relevant messages or screenshots. You do not need to gather evidence before asking for help.

Reports go to the organisation you choose, not to this website. You can still get support without making a police report.

Your voice can make a difference

Why you should report

Reporting hate can help you and your community. It is your choice, and you can take things at your own pace.

01 / ACCESS SUPPORT

Get the help you need

A report can help connect you with advice, practical support and services that understand what you have experienced.

02 / BUILD A PICTURE

Help identify patterns

Reports help police and local services understand where hate is happening and recognise repeated incidents.

03 / SUPPORT CHANGE

Help make Leeds safer

Sharing what happened can inform prevention and local responses. It may help protect others from similar harm.

You are not to blame. You do not have to report to receive support.

Reporting does not guarantee an investigation or a particular outcome. A support worker can explain your options before you decide.

Let’s make sense of it

Not sure if it was
a hate crime?

Hate crime is a crime involving hostility towards someone’s race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or transgender identity. Discrimination can be unlawful even when it is not a crime.

It might happen in person or online. Even if you’re unsure what to call it, you can still seek support.

Understand hate crime & your rights →

It can take different forms

  • Threats, harassment or verbal abuse
  • Physical attacks or damage to property
  • Abusive messages or online targeting

Whatever happened, it wasn’t your fault.

01 / What the words mean

01

Hate crime

A criminal offence involving hostility based on race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or transgender identity. It can happen in person or online.

Examples include assault, criminal damage or harassment. The hostility can relate to who someone thinks you are, even if they are mistaken.

02

Non-crime hate incident

An incident involving hostility or prejudice where no criminal offence is identified. You may hear the abbreviation “NCHI”. It is not a criminal conviction.

Police decide whether an incident meets the recording rules. Feeling offended alone is not enough; lawful debate and expression are protected.

03

Discrimination

Unfair treatment linked to a protected characteristic. The Equality Act protects people in settings such as work, education, housing and public services.

Not all unfair treatment is unlawful. The facts, the setting and the legal rules matter. Discrimination can be unlawful without being a crime.

You can still ask for help if no crime is recorded.

Police recording rules are changing. Ask the police or an adviser how the current rules apply to your situation.

A little reassurance

It’s okay to
have questions.

You’re in control of what happens next.

Do I have to report it to get support?
No. You can contact a support service to talk about what happened without making a police report.
What if I’m not sure it was a hate crime?
You don’t need to have all the answers. A specialist service can help you understand your options.
Can I get help for someone else?
Yes. If you witnessed an incident or are supporting a friend or family member, you can ask for advice.

Need help reading or communicating?

You can ask support services about an interpreter, accessible information or communication support. Tell them what works for you.
